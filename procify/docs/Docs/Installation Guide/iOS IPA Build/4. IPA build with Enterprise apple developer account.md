# IPA build with Enterprise apple developer account

## 2.1. Create a Certificates for your Project

### 2.1.1.Create a key chain

A **.certSigningRequest** file is a critical part of the **certificate generation process** in iOS development. It is used to create a **code-signing certificate**, which is necessary for signing your iOS apps for distribution via **In-House** or **Ad Hoc** methods. The CSR file contains information about the developer or the organization, including the public key, which is used by Apple to generate the appropriate signing certificate.If you want to create a new **Certificate,** then follow the steps below.

- Open the keychain Access as shown in the below screenshot.

> ![Screenshot](./img/ios-ipa-build-image453.png)

- Click on **Request a Certificate From a Certificate Authority** as shown in the below screenshot.

> ![Screenshot](./img/ios-ipa-build-image287.png)

- Click on **Keychain Access** as shown in the below screenshot.

> ![Screenshot](./img/ios-ipa-build-image439.png)

- Fill the below details as shown in the below screenshot.

  - The user's email address plays an indirect role in the code-signing process since it is linked to your Apple Developer account and included in the signing certificate's metadata. While the Keychain holds the signing certificate and private key, it does not store the email address in plain text. Instead, the email is embedded within the certificate's metadata, which identifies the developer or organization.

  - **In short:**

<!-- -->

- Email is used to identify the developer in the signing certificate.

- Keychain stores the certificate and private key needed for signing the app, enabling secure app distribution and code signing.

  - The **CA Email Address** is a part of the Issuer information within a certificate and provides a way to contact the Certificate Authority that issued the certificate. It serves as a point of contact for issues related to the certificate, such as validation, renewal, or revocation. You can find the CA's email address in the certificate metadata, which can be inspected in various tools like Keychain Access on macOS or by viewing the certificate details in a web browser.

### 2.1.2.Certificate the Certificates

- **Certificates** ensure your app is properly authorized for development, testing, and distribution. They act as a security mechanism to ensure that only authorized developers and apps can interact with Apple devices. Without these certificates, your app cannot be installed on devices or distributed via the App Store.

- If you want to create a new **Certificate,** then follow the steps below.

1.  Open your Apple developer account.

2.  Click on **Certificates** as shown in the below screenshot

> ![Screenshot](./img/ios-ipa-build-image369.png)

3.  Click on the **create ( + )** button as shown below.

> ![Screenshot](./img/ios-ipa-build-image450.png)

4.  Select the **In-House and Ad Hoc** check box and click on **continue.**

> **In-House**: Choose this if you need to distribute apps internally within a company, especially if the app is for internal use and will be installed on many devices within your organization. This is the best choice for enterprise-level apps and internal tools.
>
> **Ad Hoc**: Choose Ad Hoc distribution if you have a small group of testers or specific clients who need to test or review your app before release. This is the best choice for beta testing or distributing an app to a limited number of users.
>
> **Both methods are critical for scenarios where an app is not intended for public release via the App Store but needs to be tested or used by a controlled group.**
>
> ![Screenshot](./img/ios-ipa-build-image267.png)

5.  In the next screen select the certificate request file (a file with a [**<u>.certSigningRequest</u>**](#create-a-key-chain) file extension), then click Choose and click on continue.

6.  Now you can see the Certificate in the list as shown below,

### 2.1.3.Create a key

- The **Bundle ID** is a unique identifier for your iOS app, and its uses in app development and distribution are crucial for various processes:

  - **<u>App Identification</u>**: Differentiates your app from others in the Apple ecosystem and **App Store**.

  - **<u>Code Signing and Provisioning Profiles</u>**: Ties the app to **provisioning profiles** for code signing.

  - **<u>Enabling Services</u>**: Used to enable services like **Push Notifications**, **iCloud**, **In-App Purchases**, **Game Center**, and more.

  - <u>**Managing Versions**:</u> Helps to manage different versions of the app, such as production and staging.

  - <u>**App Group and Keychain Sharing**:</u> Facilitates data sharing between multiple apps.

  - <u>**App Distribution**:</u> Ensures correct signing and submission for **App Store** or **TestFlight**.

  - <u>**App Extensions**:</u> Ties extensions (e.g., widgets, share extensions) to the main app.

  - <u>**Security**:</u> Ensures app integrity during installation and distribution.

### 2.1.5.Create identifiers

- **Identifiers** play a central role in iOS app development, as they are used to uniquely identify different components and services across the **Apple ecosystem**. They are essential for ensuring proper code signing, testing, distribution, and enabling services like **Push Notifications**, **In-App Purchases**, and **iCloud**.If you want to create new **Identifiers,** then follow the steps below.

- Click on **( + )** button as shown in the below screenshot.

> ![Screenshot](./img/ios-ipa-build-image278.png)

- In the **Apple Developer Portal**, **App IDs** are essential for associating your iOS app with Apple's developer services. By creating either **Explicit App IDs** or **Wildcard App IDs**, you ensure that your app is properly configured for code signing, provisioning profiles, and services such as **Push Notifications** and **In-App Purchases**. It's critical to select the right type of App ID based on your app’s needs and how you intend to distribute it (via App Store, Ad Hoc, or In-House).

- Select **App IDs** as shown in the below screenshot.

> ![Screenshot](./img/ios-ipa-build-image454.png)

- Provide the details and scroll down

  - The **Description** field in the **Identifiers & Profiles** section of the **Apple Developer Portal** is a valuable tool for organizing, managing, and tracking your app development and distribution assets. It improves clarity and helps developers and teams keep track of multiple configurations, ensuring smooth app development and distribution workflows.

  - The **Bundle ID** is a critical part of the iOS development and distribution process. It uniquely identifies your app, associates it with your developer account, and enables it to interact with various **Apple services**. Properly setting and managing your **Bundle ID** is essential for a smooth app development workflow, particularly when managing app versions, provisioning profiles, and services like **push notifications** and **in-app purchase**

> ![Screenshot](./img/ios-ipa-build-image329.png)

- Click on the **Push Notification** check box and click on **continue**.

  - **Push notifications** are an incredibly powerful tool for engaging users, delivering timely information, and enhancing user retention. By leveraging **Apple Push Notification Service (APNs)**, iOS developers can integrate push notifications into their apps to send personalized, real-time updates to users. However, it's important to use push notifications thoughtfully, respecting user preferences and ensuring the content is relevant and non-intrusive. When implemented properly, push notifications can significantly improve user experience, drive engagement, and increase the overall success of an app.
